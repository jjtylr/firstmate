# Evidence grade for the reflect-back move

Read this before citing any move in `SKILL.md` as established. The short version: **the core move is
practice-backed, not evidence-backed.** It ships as a menu to try, not a protocol to obey.

## What the core move rests on

Two independent fields converged on the same instrument, which is the strongest warrant available
here — and convergent practice is not the same thing as evidence.

- **Survey methodology — the paraphrasing probe.** _"Can you repeat the question I just asked in your
  own words?"_ (Willis 1999, cognitive interviewing for questionnaire design). Diagnoses the
  _question_.
- **Clinical care — teach-back.** _"Tell me in your own words…"_ Diagnoses the _explanation_.

Both insist on _own words_. Both warn that parroting is a false pass. **Neither field has validated
it.**

## Where the evidence is strong, it is for a different task

The forensic Cognitive Interview shows **d = 1.20** for correct details recalled (Memon, Meissner &
Fraser 2010 — meta-analysis, 46 articles, N = 2,887). That is **recalling a witnessed event**, not
**articulating a want you cannot name**.

**DERIVED:** that the retrieval mechanics transfer from event-recall to want-articulation is our own
inference. It is not an established result and must not be presented as one.

## Grading elsewhere is thin

- Teach-back is endorsed by AHRQ, NQF and AAFP, and graded by essentially none of them. AAFP rates it
  **SORT level C** — expert opinion / usual practice.
- Its foundational study (Schillinger et al. 2003) rests on **12 exposed visits**, with a confidence
  interval spanning a factor of roughly 68.
- Survey cognitive interviewing's inter-interviewer reliability runs **r = .14–.33**.

## Where each move in `SKILL.md` came from

| Move | Source |
|---|---|
| Take responsibility before reflecting | 5Ts operational definition (Anderson, Leister & De Rego 2020) — a _tell me_ line without a _take responsibility_ line first puts the burden on the interviewee |
| Transfer control at the open; ~20% of the talking | Forensic CI's transfer-of-control instruction, plus its 80-20 rule |
| Chunk and check | Teach-back — _"do not wait until the end to initiate teach-back"_ |
| Own words; a parrot is a false pass | Both traditions, stated explicitly in each |
| Specific reflections beat general ones | Teach-back — _"tell me what you learned about diabetes"_ fails; _"how will you use your glucose meter when you go home?"_ works |
| Triage to 1–3 topics | Survey CI — named as the key to efficiency, because it stops the interviewer dumping everything |
| Vary the route; never re-ask | Forensic CI — _"multiple searches of memory should lead to more finds, but only if different access routes are explored"_ |
| Weight late arrivals lower | Odinot et al. 2013 — second-pass items 71% accurate against an 84% first-pass baseline |

## Termination — three traditions that disagree usefully

| Tradition | Rule |
|---|---|
| Teach-back | A genuine termination _condition_ — loop until they describe it correctly in their own words |
| Survey CI | Explicitly **anti-saturation** — 4 interviews can be a round, 12–15 max, 3–4 rounds; a single interview can be dispositive; stop on the first major problem and go revise |
| Forensic CI | None at all, deliberately — it keeps the interview open past its end |

`SKILL.md` takes teach-back's per-concept condition inside survey CI's round budget. **Which
combination actually works is exactly what running it will tell us.**

## Still unresolved

**Contamination vs. activation.** Paraphrasing in _new_ words triggers the re-read but imposes your
framing; mirroring in the user's _own_ words avoids leading but may trigger nothing. The research did
not settle it. `SKILL.md`'s answer — paraphrase, but where a term is load-bearing offer a concrete
either/or rather than picking — is **provisional and untested**.
